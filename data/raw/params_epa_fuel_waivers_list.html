Fuel Waivers

EPA, working with the Department of Energy, responds quickly to address fuel supply disruptions caused by, for example, refinery or pipeline infrastructure damage as the result of a hurricane or other natural disaster, by issuing emergency waivers of certain fuel standards in affected areas. When issued, these temporary waivers are necessary to help ensure that an adequate supply of fuel is available, particularly for emergency vehicle needs. There were no waivers issued in 2009, 2010, 2013 and 2015.

The waiver temporarily lifts federal ultra-low sulfur diesel (ULSD, 15 ppm) requirements to allow the use of higher sulfur diesel in certain older diesel vehicles and nonroad equipment originally designed and certified for the fuel. The waiver will have an effective window from October 5, 2026, through October 24,2026, which is the maximum 20 days allotted under the CAA, and is only eligible for vehicles and equipment operating above the Arctic Circle. On August 20, 2026, EPA, in consultation with U.S. Department of Energy and in accordance with the Clean Air Act, issued a temporary waiver to address extreme and unusual fuel supply circumstances affecting all parts of the United States. This action is a renewal of the August 9, 2026, waiver extension. This action extends the ethanol blending limit in gasoline from 10 percent to 15 percent, reinstates the 1 psi allowance for ethanol blends in states where it was previously removed, and waives federal low volatility gasoline standards in two steps. In the first step, it extends the existing volatility waiver through August 31, 2026, maintaining a single national gasoline pool with 9 to 15 percent ethanol content at a common Reid Vapor Pressure standard of 10 psi. In the second step, it waives all Federal volatility standards starting on September 1, 2026, and pulls forward the wintertime volatility limits that would otherwise apply at the end of the summertime control season.
