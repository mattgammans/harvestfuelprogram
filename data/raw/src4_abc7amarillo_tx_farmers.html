Texas farmers get temporary dyed diesel, truck weight relief during harvest

by Drew Powell

Tue, September 29, 2026 at 6:38 PM
Updated Tue, September 29, 2026 at 7:00 PM

Texas farmers and ranchers are in the middle of the fall harvest season, and a temporary state order is expected to provide some financial relief for those hauling agricultural products.

Gov. Greg Abbott issued a statewide disaster proclamation allowing the expanded use of dyed diesel on Texas roads and temporarily waiving certain weight restrictions for trucks hauling agricultural commodities.

Jared Blankenship, an agricultural producer and Texas Farm Bureau District 1 state director, said producers appreciate the recognition of the financial challenges they face, even if the relief is temporary.

“First impression is that we're appreciative of the recognition that there is a problem,” Blankenship said. “While a temporary measure, just a little relief is a positive measure.”

Jeffery Carlson, an agricultural producer, said the order comes during a busy time for farmers harvesting crops.

“We're currently in our grain harvest time of the year,” Carlson said. “We're currently harvesting corn, which we're three-fourths done with. We have about three or four more days of harvest to go. I know for a lot of people this will help short term. Any little bit will help.”

The temporary weight waiver applies to trucks hauling agricultural commodities, including silage, corn and grain sorghum.

Blankenship said the savings may be modest for individual trips but can still help producers manage expenses during harvest.

“To put into some perspective, if you're talking vehicles, that amounts to maybe $15 on a pickup and around $100 on trucks,” Blankenship said.

Carlson said the relief is especially welcome for producers hauling grain to elevators and for those transporting silage.

According to the Texas Farm Bureau, the financial relief is expected to last 30 days.
