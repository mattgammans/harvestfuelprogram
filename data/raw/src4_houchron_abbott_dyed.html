Greg Abbott lifts restrictions on dyed diesel in effort to curb high fuel prices

By Benjamin Wermund, Staff WriterSep 28, 2026

Gov. Greg Abbott on Monday waived restrictions on diesel typically allowed only in off-road vehicles, part of an effort to curb skyrocketing gas prices amid a growing diesel shortage. 

The Republican governor issued a disaster proclamation expanding the use of dyed diesel, an off-road fuel typically banned from public roadways because it is exempt from fuel taxes. Abbott also wrote to the Environmental Protection Agency requesting a Clean Air Act waiver to skirt federal limits on which diesel can be sold.  

“Texas agriculture and freight run on diesel,” Abbott said in a statement. “Record prices put both industries at risk and raise costs for every Texas family. Farmers and truckers can now use dyed diesel on Texas roads, and fuel, crop, and timber loads can move at a higher weight. These steps cut costs on the farm, on the road, and at the store."

The move to free up more diesel comes as Abbott is locked in a close reelection contest ahead of a midterm dominated by cost of living concerns. Abbott in July predicted gas prices would fall “below $2 before the end of summer.” They have climbed since, with diesel nearing $6 per gallon across the state. 

Gina Hinojosa, Abbott’s Democratic challenger, called on the governor to waive taxes on diesel earlier this month. His office had dismissed those calls, saying Abbott did not have the power to unilaterally waive taxes. 

Abbott has previously eased restrictions on dyed diesel, including during 2024 wildfires in the Panhandle.
