Diesel prices top $6 a gallon, straining truckers and farmers

Governor pushes 90-day export ban as farmers turn to dyed diesel
By Colby Wilkerson
Published: Sep. 24, 2026 at 6:19 PM CDT
Add Us On GoogleAdd as a preferred source on Google

SHREVEPORT, La. (KSLA) - Diesel prices have hit an all-time high, topping $6 a gallon. KSLA has learned from sources that some local businesses have completely run out of supply.

Truckers feel the squeeze

For truckers who live by the price at the pump, $6 diesel isn’t just an inconvenience, it’s a crisis.

Chris Thornton, owner of Thornton Logistics, said the spike is impacting his business and his family at the same time.

“It’s cutting our profits in half on a lot of stuff, like, you know, 143 gallons would normally cost us around three to four hundred dollars, and it’s killing us. I mean, we’re literally, our profits are cut in half, and that’s a lot of money out of our families, out of food, out of our family’s mouths,” Thornton said.

Farmers absorbing rising costs

Truckers aren’t the only ones feeling the squeeze. LSU Ag associate extension agent Joshua Salley said farmers across Louisiana are absorbing the same rising costs, and that pain doesn’t stop at the farm gate.

“On the hay production side, I know a lot of my hay producers here in Desoto Parish that sell hay, they had no option but to go up on their prices for this year just to kind of offset that added expense of fuel,” Salley said.

Governor signs order for dyed diesel

To help ease the burden, Gov. Jeff Landry signed an executive order allowing farmers to use dyed diesel. Salley said the fuel itself is the same as regular diesel.

“Is exactly the same fuel as highway diesel, it’s just tinted with a red dye. And what that does is it shows that it’s a tax-exempt product and it’s legally restricted to off-road equipment only,” Salley said.

Push for export ban

Landry is also pushing for a bigger fix: a 90-day ban on U.S. diesel exports. He said the pause could bring prices down for families, farmers and small businesses alike.

“What I’m saying is, look, give it a 90-day pause. Let’s let that supply fill up. Let diesel prices come down, especially during harvest time for our particular farmers,” Landry said.
