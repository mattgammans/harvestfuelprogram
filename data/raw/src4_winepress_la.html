Louisiana Becomes First To Declare State Of Emergency To Secure Diesel Supply For Farmers Amidst Skyrocketing Costs

“We’re not going to sit on the sidelines while Louisiana farmers are paying record prices to harvest the crops that feed our families and support our economy.”

The WinePress
Sep 24, 2026

Louisiana Governor Jeff Landry has become the first to declare a State of Emergency, with probably more to come, in an attempt to address the energy crisis being felt in the state and across the U.S., particularly with farmers who are being forced to deal with skyrocketing diesel costs.

According to a press release from the Governor’s desk signed on September 22nd, the post reads:

Governor Landry has issued an Executive Order declaring a state of emergency, allowing Louisiana farmers and timber harvesters to use the dyed diesel fuel they already keep on hand. The order comes at the peak of harvest season, with diesel at the highest price ever recorded in this state. EO attached.

“We’re not going to sit on the sidelines while Louisiana farmers are paying record prices to harvest the crops that feed our families and support our economy. We have an opportunity to provide immediate relief, and that’s exactly what we’re doing,” said Gov. Landry.

Dyed diesel is ordinary diesel fuel dyed red to mark it as off-road fuel. Farmers and loggers buy it for tractors, skidders, and irrigation pumps, and state law has long barred them from burning it in a licensed highway vehicle. This Executive Order suspends the penalty through October 22, 2026 for vehicles registered as Class 2 (Forest Products) or Class 5 (Farm Use) during this time of peak harvesting operations.

The Order also directs the Secretary of the Louisiana Department of Revenue to request federal penalty relief from the IRS by the end of the week.

The diesel shortage driving these high prices is a national issue. U.S. inventories of distillate fuel oil are currently 12.5 percent below the five-year average, contributing to higher domestic diesel prices. At the same time, U.S. net exports of distillate fuel oil remain near five-year highs. In Louisiana, the average retail price of diesel has reached an all-time high of $6.03 per gallon, 112 percent higher than the price the LSU AgCenter used to develop its 2026 crop enterprise budgets.

As noted by the Shreveport Times, “Dyed diesel is cheaper than regular diesel because it is exempt from state and federal highway excise taxes and generally allowed only to be used on off-road equipment like tractors.”

Louisiana Farm Bureau President Richard Fontenot lauded the move. “Anything helps, especially as we work to get the crops out of the fields,” Fontenot said in an interview with USA Today. “I’m appreciative of the governor’s efforts to give us any relief during this time.”

The American Sugar Cane League also thanked the Governor for signing the EO.

“Louisiana sugarcane farmers are receiving much-needed relief from record-high diesel prices as the 2026 harvest gets underway following an executive order signed by Governor Jeff Landry declaring an emergency due to the shortage and high cost of diesel fuel.”

As expressed in an interview with CNBC, Landry, an ally of President Trump’s, has also supported a temporary diesel export ban, something Trump and other administration officials have endorsed.

While not official, yet, it increasingly appears that perhaps the White House will enact some sort of a diesel export ban, despite the seemingly unanimous sentiment coming from the oil industry that this will do little to curb prices, perhaps temporarily domestically, but will only cause prices to climb even higher over the long term.
