North Dakota lets farmers run cheaper dyed diesel on highways

By AGDAILY Published: September 30, 2026

Share Tweet Pin
A combine makes a pass in Colfax, North Dakota. (Image by tony Franzwa, Shutterstock)

North Dakota Gov. Kelly Armstrong declared a state of emergency on Tuesday over the supply and availability of diesel fuel, issuing an executive order that temporarily broadens how red-dyed diesel may be used to give agricultural producers relief during harvest.

Under state law, North Dakota taxes regular diesel at 23 cents per gallon but charges only 4 cents per gallon on red-dyed diesel, a fuel typically limited to certain off-highway agricultural, industrial, and railroad purposes. The order lets farmers put that lower-taxed fuel to broader use.

Armstrong’s executive order broadens dyed-diesel use to cover licensed motor vehicles operated as part of ag operations. That allows farmers to burn the cheaper fuel not only in tractors and harvesting equipment for field work but also in on-highway roles like moving grain, livestock, feed, seed, fertilizer, and equipment, along with storing and processing ag products. 

The order took effect immediately and runs through Nov. 30. It leaves the federal 24.4-cent-per-gallon diesel excise tax untouched.

The average price for diesel fuel in North Dakota was $6.17 on Tuesday, according to AAA.

“Farmers burn more diesel during harvest than at any other time of year, and saving 19 cents per gallon can make a meaningful difference when margins are razor thin. This translates into lower transportation costs to suppliers and processors, which hopefully will be passed on to the consumer,” said Agriculture Commissioner Doug Goehring. “I remain committed to working with the governor and the ag community to identify ways to provide additional relief for producers during this time of high input costs and low commodity prices.”

The North Dakota Monitor reported federal stats that show about 22 percent of the state’s soybeans had been harvested as of Sunday and that corn harvest was 8 percent complete.
