WHEAT: The outlook for 2026/27 U.S. wheat this month is for higher supplies, increased
domestic use, reduced exports, and greater ending stocks. Supplies are raised by 8 million
bushels on higher imports and production. The NASS Small Grains Annual Summary
estimated wheat production slightly greater at 1,534 million bushels on an increased yield
more than offsetting reduced harvested area. Despite the increase, this remains the smallest
U.S. wheat production since 1970. Domestic use is raised 10 million bushels, all on higher
feed and residual use. The NASS Grain Stocks report implied larger-than-previously-
expected first quarter (June-August) domestic disappearance. Exports are reduced 25 million
bushels to 750 million on lower sales and shipments to date for Hard Red Winter (HRW) and
Hard Red Spring that are partially offset by increased White exports. HRW is the largest
decline as HRW continues to be uncompetitive in export markets with significantly higher
prices than last year. Projected all wheat ending stocks are raised by 23 million bushels to
740 million but are still 20 percent below last year. The season-average farm price is lowered
$0.10 per bushel to $6.30 on increased stocks, NASS prices reported to date, and
expectations for futures and cash prices for the remainder of the marketing year.

This month’s 2026/27 global wheat outlook is for higher supplies, greater consumption,
reduced trade, and slightly lower ending stocks. Supplies are projected up 0.9 million tons to
1,103.9 million on increased beginning stocks mainly for Australia. Global production is only
minimally revised with offsetting changes for several countries. EU production is lowered 0.8
million tons to 133.0 million, mostly on a reduction for Germany. Canada and Turkey
production are each raised 0.5 million tons to 36.5 million and 23.0 million, respectively, on
higher yields. Global consumption is raised 1.2 million tons to 827.9 million, primarily on
higher feed and residual use for China and the EU. World trade is 2.8 million tons lower at
209.0 million as reductions for Russia and the United States are only partially offset by
increased exports for Argentina and Canada. Russia’s exports are now the lowest in 5 years
at 40.0 million tons with logistical disruptions caused by the continuing conflict in the Black
Sea region reducing shipments to date well below their long-term average. Projected
2026/27 global ending stocks are fractionally lower at 276.0 million tons with decreases for
the EU, China, Argentina, Egypt, and several other countries nearly offset by increases for
Russia, Australia, and the United States.

COARSE GRAINS: This month’s 2026/27 U.S. corn outlook is for larger supplies, exports,
domestic use, and ending stocks. Projected beginning stocks for 2026/27 are raised 173
million bushels based on the September 30 Grain Stocks report. Corn production is higher as
well, up 234 million bushels to 16.0 billion on a 2.7-bushel increase in yield to 181.2 bushels
per acre. Harvested area for grain is unchanged at 88.5 million acres. Total use is increased
125 million bushels to 16.3 billion. Each component of use is increased. Exports are raised
25 million bushels to 3.3 billion on increased supplies. With supply increased more than use,

ending stocks rise by 282 million bushels to 1.8 billion. The season-average corn price
received by producers is lowered $0.10 per bushel to $4.70.

Global coarse grain production for 2026/27 is forecast 1.0 million tons lower to 1.589 billion.
This month’s foreign coarse grain outlook is for lower production and use but larger stocks
relative to last month with virtually unchanged trade. Foreign corn production is expected to
decline, primarily in the EU, the Philippines, and Mexico. EU corn production is lowered on a
combination of harvest results and revised government estimates, particularly in France
where production is forecast 38 percent below the prior year.

Major foreign coarse grain trade changes for 2026/27 include larger corn imports for the
Philippines and Egypt but a reduction for Vietnam. Barley exports are cut for Argentina and
Russia. Foreign corn ending stocks are higher relative to last month, mostly reflecting an
increase in Argentina. World corn ending stocks, at 280.4 million tons, are up 8.3 million tons
primarily reflecting larger stocks for the United States.

RICE: The outlook for 2026/27 U.S. rice this month is for smaller supplies, unchanged
domestic use and exports, and lower ending stocks. Supplies are decreased all on production,
which is lowered to 157.7 million cwt, all on reduced yields as indicated by the NASS October
Crop Production report. The average all-rice yield is down 22 pounds per acre to 7,668
pounds. All-rice 2026/27 ending stocks are projected 0.4 million cwt lower at 40.0 million and
are down 32 percent from last year. The 2026/27 all-rice season-average farm price (SAFP) is
projected up $0.30 per cwt at $15.20. This increase is on a higher long-grain SAFP, which is
raised $0.50 per cwt to $14.00 based on observed long-grain cash and futures prices to date.

The 2026/27 global rice outlook this month is for higher supplies, reduced consumption and
trade, and increased ending stocks. Global supplies are raised 0.7 million tons to 737.3 million
as higher beginning stocks for India, Japan, and Bangladesh more than offset lower production
for the Philippines, Pakistan, and Russia. World 2026/27 consumption is decreased 0.8 million
tons to 538.8 million, largely on reductions for several Asian and African countries. Global
2026/27 trade is 1.3 million tons lower at 61.4 million tons, mainly on reduced India exports.
Projected 2026/27 world ending stocks are raised 1.5 million tons to 198.5 million on increases
for India and Japan that are only partially offset by reductions for China, the Philippines, and
Indonesia.

OILSEEDS: U.S. oilseed production for 2026/27 is forecast at 133.6 million tons, up 0.7
million from last month with higher soybean and cottonseed production partly offset by lower
peanut, canola, and sunflowerseed output. Soybean production is forecast at 4.56 billion
bushels, up 27 million on higher yields. Harvested area is unchanged at 85.9 million acres.
The soybean yield is projected at 53.1 bushels per acre, up 0.3 bushels from the September
forecast. Soybean supplies are raised 17 million bushels to 4.90 billion on higher production
partly offset by lower beginning stocks. With a 10-million-bushel increase to exports and
unchanged crush, ending stocks are up 5 million bushels to 315 million.

Prices are unchanged for 2026/27. The U.S. season-average soybean price is forecast at
$12.00 per bushel; soybean meal and oil prices are projected at $340 per short ton and 70
cents per pound, respectively.

The global soybean supply and demand forecasts for 2026/27 include lower beginning
stocks, higher production, raised crush and exports, and increased ending stocks. Beginning

stocks are reduced for Argentina on raised crush for the prior marketing year and on lower
stocks for the United States. Global soybean production is increased 0.4 million tons to 442.7
million on higher production for the United States. Partly offsetting is lower production for
India and the EU.

Global 2026/27 crush is increased on higher crush for Argentina and Thailand in line with
upward revisions to crush in the prior year. Global exports are increased on raised exports
for the United States and Nigeria. Global soybean ending stocks are increased slightly by 0.3
million tons to 124.3 million. Another notable oilseed change includes a reduction of palm oil
production for Indonesia. Indonesia palm oil production is lowered 2.2 million tons to 45
million based on dry weather over the past several months lowering crop prospects ahead.
Global palm oil production for 2026/27 is forecast to decline 2.5 million tons from the prior
marketing year, with global stocks the lowest since 2017/18.

SUGAR: An increase in U.S. sugar supply for 2025/26, combined with a reduction in use,
increases U.S. ending stocks 199,000 short tons, raw value (STRV) to 2.231 million. Cane
sugar production in Louisiana is increased 53,000 STRV on higher September production
than projected last month. September production is estimated at 119,945 STRV. Partially
offsetting is a reduction in beet sugar production of 37,730 STRV on lower early season
production in August and September projected by processors in the Sweetener Market Data
(SMD). Imports are increased 133,754 STRV, mostly on raw sugar high-tier tariff imports (up
91,608 STRV to 606,057) and re-export imports (up 58,696 STRV to 398,831). Use is
decreased 49,615 STRV as deliveries for human consumption are lowered to 12.392 million
on a slowdown in the pace to date.

U.S. sugar supply for 2026/27 is increased to 14.387 million STRV as increased beginning
stocks and lower use are only partially offset by lower production. Beet sugar production is
decreased 27,200 STRV to 4.742 million reflecting lower national sugarbeet yield reported by
NASS, in addition to the early production change (see above). Louisiana cane sugar
production is decreased to 2.208 million STRV mostly on higher September production for
2025/26 from the current sugarcane crop. There is a partial offset from processors increasing
their percentage of sugarcane area for sugar over for seed. Florida sugar production is
unchanged based on processors’ reporting in the SMD. Imports are only slightly lower than
last month. Projected deliveries for human consumption are carried over from 2025/26
lowering use by 49,615 STRV. Ending stocks are projected at 1.865 million STRV for an
ending stocks-to-use ratio of 14.9 percent, up from 13.5 percent last month.

Mexico sugar production for 2025/26 and 2026/27 is unchanged from last month. Deliveries
for human consumption for both years are increased by small amounts based on FAS Mexico
City Post reporting. Exports for 2025/26 are increased to 1.00 million MT based on Post
reporting, and ending stocks are decreased to 1.184 million metric tons (MT). The reduction
of 2026/27 beginning stocks of 185,652 MT implies lower total exports and a small reduction
in ending stocks. Exports under license to the United States at 1.017 million MT are
unchanged from last month. Ending stocks for both years are estimated to have 150,000 MT
of below 99.2 polarity sugar intended for export to the United States.

LIVESTOCK, POULTRY, AND DAIRY: The forecast for 2026 red meat and poultry production
is lowered from last month as lower beef and pork production is partly offset by increased
broiler and turkey production. Beef production is lowered for the second half as lower
expected steer and heifer slaughter and lighter carcass weights more than offset higher cow
                                         WASDE-676-4

slaughter. Pork production is lowered on a slower expected pace of slaughter and lighter
dressed weights. Broiler and turkey production are raised on current slaughter, weight, and
hatchery data. Egg production is increased on growth in the layer flock.

For 2027, the red meat and poultry production forecast is reduced as lower beef and pork
production more than offset higher broiler and turkey production. Beef production is lowered
on lighter dressed weights as well as lower-than-expected feedlot placements in the second
half of 2026 that are expected to slow marketings during the first quarter of 2027. The
USDA’s Quarterly Hogs and Pigs report, released on September 24, estimated a lower pig
crop for June-August and lower farrowing intentions for September-November. This supports
lower hog slaughter expectations for the first half of 2027. Broiler and turkey production are
raised on higher expected slaughter. Egg production is increased during the first half of the
year on growth in the layer flock.

Beef imports are raised in 2026, primarily on recent strength from Australia. Imports are also
raised for the second half of 2027 on increased global supplies. Beef exports are unchanged
for 2026 and 2027. Pork exports are lowered for both 2026 and 2027 on reduced domestic
production and weakness in shipments to Western Hemisphere markets. Broiler exports are
raised for both 2026 and 2027 on stronger-than-expected demand from the Caribbean.
Turkey exports are increased in 2026 and 2027 on recent pace of exports and increased
production. Egg exports are decreased in 2026 on updated trade data but are unchanged in
2027.

Cattle price forecasts were unchanged for 2026 and 2027. Hog price forecasts are
decreased for the remainder of 2026 and the first half of 2027 on persistent weakness in the
pork cutout. Broiler price forecasts are lowered in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the second
half of 2027 on increased domestic supplies and trends in demand seasonality. Turkey price
forecasts for 2026 and 2027 are raised on current price strength and tight supplies of whole
birds. Egg price forecasts are lowered for the remainder of 2026 on recent price weakness
and increased production but are unchanged for 2027.

The milk production forecast is decreased slightly for both 2026 and 2027. Based on the
latest Milk Production report, cow inventories were lowered for 2026 and output per cow is
lowered for both 2026 and 2027.

For both 2026 and 2027, commercial exports are lowered on a fat basis due to lower-than-
expected shipments of butter and cheese. Commercial exports are virtually unchanged for
2026 on a skim-solids basis but lowered slightly in 2027 due to lower expected shipments of
dry whey. Also, for both 2026 and 2027, commercial imports are raised on a fat basis on
increased shipments of butter. Commercial imports on a skim-solids basis are unchanged in
2026 and 2027.

For both 2026 and 2027, butter and cheese price forecasts are decreased based on recent
prices and growth in butterfat production. The NDM and whey price forecasts are raised to
reflect recent prices and strong demand for protein products. For both 2026 and 2027, the
Class III price forecast is lowered as higher whey prices only partially offset lower cheese
prices, and the Class IV price forecast is raised as higher NDM prices more than fully offset
lower butter prices. For 2026, the all milk price forecast is unchanged at $19.90 per cwt. For
2027, however, the all milk price forecast is increased to $20.45 per cwt.

COTTON: The 2026/27 U.S. cotton balance sheet shows higher production and ending
stocks compared to last month. Projected consumption, exports, imports, and beginning
stocks are unchanged. The production forecast is increased 2 percent to 13.44 million bales
as the projected national average yield is also raised 2 percent to 790 pounds per harvested
acre. Larger crops for all Delta States drive the increase, with changes in other States largely
offsetting each other. With the larger U.S. crop, the forecast for ending stocks is raised 6
percent to 3.80 million bales, for a stocks-to-use ratio of 27.5 percent, up from 26.1 percent
last month. The projected season-average farm price for upland cotton is lowered 1 cent from
last month to 77 cents/lb.

There are no changes to the 2025/26 U.S. cotton balance sheet. The season-average farm
price for 2025/26 is raised marginally to 62.2 cents/lb.

Projected world production, consumption, trade, and beginning and ending stocks for
2026/27 are all increased. Production is over 600,000 bales higher this month driven by
increases for Brazil and the United States partially offset by lower production in Argentina.
Consumption is raised by just over 160,000 bales, driven by increases for Vietnam, Malaysia,
Guatemala, and Nicaragua, partially offset by small decreases elsewhere. Projected exports
are up by almost 500,000 bales because of higher exports from Brazil with small and largely
offsetting changes for other countries. Global ending stocks are up by over 800,000 bales
mostly because of the increase in beginning stocks along with higher ending stocks for the
United States and India.

                                   Related USDA Reports

The WASDE report incorporates information from a number of statistical reports published by USDA and
other government agencies. In turn, the WASDE report provides a framework for more detailed reports
issued by USDA’s Economic Research Service and Foreign Agricultural Service. For more information on
how the WASDE report is prepared, go to: http://www.usda.gov/oce/commodity/wasde.



                              Supply and Demand Database

The Foreign Agricultural Service publishes Production, Supply, and Demand Online, a comprehensive
database of supply and demand balances by commodity for 190 countries and regions at
https://apps.fas.usda.gov/psdonline/app/index.html. Data for grains, oilseeds, and cotton are updated
monthly and data for other commodities are updated less frequently.



                            Foreign Production Assessments

Preliminary foreign production assessments and satellite imagery analysis used to prepare the WASDE
report are provided by the International Production Assessment Division (IPAD) of the Foreign Agricultural
Service. IPAD is located at https://ipad.fas.usda.gov/.
