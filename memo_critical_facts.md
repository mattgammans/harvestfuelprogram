# Pre-modeling memo: critical facts (as of Sept. 30, 2026)

**Federal tax is untouched, and farmers still have federal exposure.** The governor's release says outright that the order "does not affect the federal government's 24.4-cent-per-gallon excise tax." EO 2026-08 §9 says it "does not authorize conduct prohibited by federal law," and users remain "responsible for compliance with all applicable federal requirements." As of Sept. 30 I found no IRS dyed-fuel penalty relief for North Dakota, or for any state in the 2026 episode. Louisiana, Arkansas, Oklahoma, Nebraska, and Alabama have publicly asked the IRS for relief. North Dakota's release does not say it has. Recent precedent points one way: Texas wildfires IR-2024-67, Hurricane Milton IR-2024-265, and earlier storms. When the IRS grants relief it waives only the §6715 penalty (the greater of $1,000 or $10/gal), and the operator must still pay the 24.4¢. So the per-gallon relief is **19¢ (state only)**, not ~43¢. That holds even if IRS relief arrives. Until it does, a farmer running dyed fuel on a public road is technically exposed to the federal penalty.

The White House is reportedly weighing a federal dyed-diesel move (Reuters, Sept. 28). It is unconfirmed and unresolved.

**EPA** is not implicated for ordinary farm fuel. Nonroad diesel has had to be 15-ppm ULSD for years (40 CFR 1090), and IRS relief is normally conditioned on ≤15 ppm dyed fuel. I found no EPA waiver for ND. Texas asked EPA for a ULSD waiver on Sept. 28, which is a different issue.

**Scope of the EO:**
- **What it suspends:** the §57-43.2-41(1) ban on dyed fuel in a licensed vehicle's tank, and the $500/$2,000/$4,000/$10,000 administrative fees. It also bars enforcement "solely on the basis of" authorized conduct.
- **Who qualifies:** any licensed vehicle used for "agricultural purposes" or for moving ag commodities, inputs, or equipment. Nothing limits it to farm-plated or farmer-owned trucks, so custom and commercial haulers appear to be covered on its face.
- **What it's silent on:** recordkeeping, IFTA, and interstate travel. It has no refund mechanism.
- **It does not expressly waive the 23¢ tax.** §8 preserves all state taxes "except to the extent expressly provided." Yet the official 19¢ savings claim depends on the tank-capacity 23¢ assessment in §57-43.2-41(1) going uncollected. In practice that looks likely, given §6. It still needs Tax Commissioner confirmation.
- **Existing refunds don't cover this.** ND has no farm-truck refund; the only 19¢ refund is for reefer units. The order therefore reaches gallons that were fully taxed before.

**Neighbors:**
- South Dakota's EO 2026-11 gives 10% weight relief only. It does not allow dyed fuel on the road, which is the SD-border commenter's problem.
- Minnesota had done nothing as of Sept. 30; a state senator has asked for it.
- Montana had only fuel-hauler hours-of-service relief.
- Other 2026 dyed-diesel actions: NE (Sept. 24, 90 days, plus an 84AG refund route), LA, AL, TX, OK, AR, and IN.
- Georgia suspended its motor fuel tax across the board instead.

**Price context:** EIA's Midwest (PADD 2) retail diesel was $6.68 in the week of Sept. 21 and $6.53 on Sept. 28. It averaged $3.62 in 2025.

**Implication for modeling:** value everything at 19¢/gal, with the state's Highway Tax Distribution Fund as the only fiscal loser. Keep federal exposure as a risk item, not a dollar offset.
